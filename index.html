What the Field Actually Teaches You

```
01
Territory Strategy
Map the neighborhood before you walk it. Identify density patterns, skip problem addresses, prioritize high-intent signals. Treat your territory like a market — not a grid to walk in sequence.
02
The 10-Second Read
You have 10 seconds on someone's porch before they decide if they'll listen. Eye contact, energy, first sentence. Mirror the homeowner's tone, don't overwhelm them, earn the next 30 seconds.
03
Trust Before Pitch
The pest control pitch that works isn't about bugs — it's about protecting the home, the kids, the foundation. Lead with what they care about, not what you're selling.
04
Objection Handling
"I already have a service" → "Let me show you something on your door frame first." "Too expensive" → "What does it cost you if we do nothing?" Every objection is a question about trust, not price.
05
Closing Under Pressure
Summer heat, rejections stacked up, end of day, quota pressure. You still have to knock the next door with the same energy as the first. Emotional regulation IS a sales skill.
06
The Follow-Up Flywheel
The second visit to a "maybe" converted more revenue than cold doors combined. The fortune is in the follow-up — in field sales and in every GTM motion that follows.

The Pitch Breakdown

Pest Control Sales Framework
Hook (10 sec): "Hey, I'm actually in your neighborhood today — I wanted to show you something real quick on your property."
Pain Reveal (60 sec): Walk them to a visible evidence point (webs, entry gaps, moisture). Make the problem real, not hypothetical.
Social Proof (30 sec): "We've been treating 14 homes on your street this week — your neighbors [name] and [name] just signed up."
Close (ask + silence): "Based on what I showed you, this makes sense for your home. Should we get you started today or Friday?"
Key Lesson That Transfers to Every GTM Role

"The close is the least important moment in the sale. The trust you built in the 4 minutes before it is everything."

This transfers directly to SaaS sales, partnership outreach, recruiting conversations, and every cold email you'll ever write.

How This Translates to Modern GTM
D2D territory mapping → ICP targeting and account segmentation
Porch pitch → cold email subject line and first sentence
Objection handling → discovery call preparation and talk tracks
Follow-up flywheel → outreach automation sequences
Closing under pressure → pipeline discipline and quota management
```

Clipboard Health Assignment

As part of exploring modern sales roles, I completed a Clipboard Health-style outreach assignment — targeting a specific ICP, building a prospect list, writing cold outreach, and thinking through the follow-up strategy.

```
Cold Email · Female-Owned Business Targeting Email · Sequence Step 1
Subject: Quick question, [First Name]

Hey [First Name],

I came across [Company Name] while researching women-owned businesses in [City] — [specific observation about their brand/mission/product].

I'm building a community and resource network specifically for women founders and operators in the [region] area. We're hosting a small roundtable next month — 15 founders, one focused conversation, no pitch decks.

Would something like that be useful to you right now?

— [Your name]
Follow-Up · Day 5 (No Reply) Email · Sequence Step 2
Subject: Re: Quick question, [First Name]

Hey [First Name] — just bumping this to the top in case it got buried.

I noticed [something new: recent post, award, news item] — congrats on that. The roundtable I mentioned earlier has spots for 2 more founders and I thought of you.

No pressure either way — just wanted to make sure it landed.

Lessons Learned

What Actually Works
  • ✦ Specificity beats length every time. One genuine observation > three generic sentences.
  • ✦ Women founders respond to community, not cold pitch. Lead with the room, not the product.
  • ✦ Follow-up with new value, not "just checking in." Give them a reason to reply.
  • ✦ Subject lines that sound like a human sent them perform 3× better than "clever" subject lines.
Female-Owned Business Targeting Strategy
Source: Certify platform, SBA database, Instagram DMs, local chamber of commerce
Filter: Founded by women, 1–20 employees, consumer or service business
Personalization: 1 real observation per email. Never templated greetings.
Track: Open rate, reply rate, conversion to conversation in HubSpot
```
